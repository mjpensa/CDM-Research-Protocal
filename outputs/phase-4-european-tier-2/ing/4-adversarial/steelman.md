## Purpose

Present the strongest possible case FOR the proposed classification (UNKNOWN). This validates the classification by showing it withstands rigorous defense.

## Steelman: Why UNKNOWN is the Correct Classification

### Argument 1: Epistemic Honesty

**The Strongest Defense:**
The research protocol demands evidence-first classification. With zero positive evidence items across all three tiers, classifying ING as anything other than UNKNOWN would be inference beyond the data.

**Supporting Points:**
- PRAGMATIST would require evidence of traditional platform usage (none found)
- OBSERVER would require evidence of ecosystem engagement (none found)
- ARCHITECT would require evidence of CDM adoption (none found)
- UNKNOWN accurately reflects our knowledge state

**Conclusion:** Intellectual honesty demands acknowledging what we don't know.

### Argument 2: Avoiding False Positives

**The Strongest Defense:**
Misclassifying ING as PRAGMATIST could lead to incorrect strategic conclusions. If ING is actually exploring CDM privately, a PRAGMATIST label would be misleading.

**Supporting Points:**
- UNKNOWN preserves optionality for future evidence
- Avoids locking in assumptions based on inference
- Signals to readers that more research is needed
- Prevents overconfidence in pattern-matching

**Conclusion:** UNKNOWN is the conservative, honest classification.

### Argument 3: Informative Absence Has Limits

**The Strongest Defense:**
While absence of evidence can be informative, it has epistemic limits. We cannot infer what ING IS doing from what they're NOT doing.

**Supporting Points:**
- Null results tell us ING isn't publicly engaged with CDM
- Null results don't tell us what platforms they use
- Null results don't tell us their strategic intent
- Null results don't tell us about internal projects

**Conclusion:** Null results support UNKNOWN, not PRAGMATIST.

### Argument 4: Low Confidence is Appropriate

**The Strongest Defense:**
30% confidence accurately reflects high uncertainty. This signals that the classification is provisional and subject to revision.

**Supporting Points:**
- Per decision-thresholds.json, Tier 4 inference caps at 35%
- 30% is within this ceiling
- Higher confidence would overstate certainty
- Low confidence invites scrutiny and future research

**Conclusion:** 30% confidence is calibrated correctly.

## Counter-Argument Rebuttals

### Rebuttal to "ING Must Be Using Something"

**Counter-argument:** "A major bank can't be unknown - they must have a solution."

**Rebuttal:** We agree ING has a solution. But knowing they have A solution doesn't tell us WHICH solution. Without evidence of the specific approach, UNKNOWN is correct for CDM classification purposes.

### Rebuttal to "Peer Banks Are PRAGMATIST"

**Counter-argument:** "Similar banks are PRAGMATIST, so ING probably is too."

**Rebuttal:** Peer comparison is useful but not conclusive. Each bank makes independent technology decisions. ING's lack of public signal could mean many things - including stealth CDM adoption.

## Final Steelman Summary

| Element | Assessment |
|---------|------------|
| Evidence base | Zero positive items - supports UNKNOWN |
| Inference limits | Cannot determine specific approach - supports UNKNOWN |
| Confidence calibration | 30% appropriate for null-results-only - supports UNKNOWN |
| Alternative classifications | Would require positive evidence we don't have |

**Verdict:** UNKNOWN (Insufficient-Evidence) at 30% confidence is the correct, defensible classification.
