# Steelman: Strongest Counter-Argument (Nomura)

## The Strongest Challenge to OBSERVER Classification

**Argument:** The JSCC CDM production launch in June 2025 creates a hard requirement for clearing member connectivity. Nomura, as a major JSCC clearing member, must have CDM connectivity to continue clearing operations. This makes them at minimum a PRAGMATIST (Regulatory-Driven) rather than an OBSERVER.

## Why This Argument Has Merit

1. **Infrastructure Mandates Trump Strategy:** When infrastructure requires CDM, banks must comply regardless of internal CDM strategy preference

2. **Timing Consideration:** JSCC went live June 2025 (6 months ago). Nomura has had time to implement connectivity solutions

3. **Binary Requirement:** Clearing members either have CDM connectivity or they do not. If Nomura is still clearing at JSCC, they must have some form of CDM connectivity

4. **Definition Precision:** OBSERVER implies "watching but not yet engaged." If Nomura has working CDM connectivity to JSCC, they are operationally engaged, which is PRAGMATIST territory

## Rebuttal

While this argument has merit, it conflates operational necessity with strategic adoption:

1. **Vendor Abstraction:** Nomura can have JSCC connectivity through vendor solutions that abstract away CDM complexity. The vendor handles CDM; Nomura interfaces with traditional messaging.

2. **Infrastructure vs Strategy:** Being required to connect to CDM infrastructure does not make a bank a CDM adopter. It makes them a user of CDM-enabled infrastructure.

3. **Sub-Classification Captures Nuance:** OBSERVER (CCP-Connected) acknowledges the infrastructure relationship while distinguishing it from strategic CDM adoption

## Verdict
This steelman argument reveals an important classification edge case. However, the OBSERVER (CCP-Connected) sub-classification appropriately captures Nomura's position: connected to CDM infrastructure by necessity, but not strategically adopting CDM for internal transformation.
