## Pillar 1: Regulatory Necessity Argument

**Argument:**

BBVA operates under EMIR Refit (EU regulatory framework). Compliance with derivatives reporting requirements is mandatory, not optional.

**Logical Chain:**

1. **EMIR Refit Requirement:**
   - Deadline: April 29, 2024 (PASSED)
   - Applies to all EU derivatives dealers
   - BBVA is Spanish Tier 1 bank (clearly in scope)

2. **Compliance Approach Necessity:**
   - Regulatory compliance is mandatory
   - Banks must choose from available approaches:
     - Option A: ISDA CDM (modern, standardized)
     - Option B: Traditional DTCC/vendor solution (legacy, compliant)
     - Option C: Regulator extension (unlikely for major bank)

3. **Observed Evidence Pattern:**
   - Zero evidence of CDM adoption
   - DTCC/vendor approach not explicitly documented (but typical)
   - No evidence of extension/non-compliance

4. **Logical Inference:**
   - BBVA must be using SOME approach
   - We observed zero CDM signals
   - Therefore: BBVA chose Option B (Traditional)
   - This pattern = PRAGMATIST (Traditional) classification

**Implication:**

UNKNOWN dodges the logical inference. When regulatory necessity dictates a choice, and one option shows zero evidence, the other option is the rational conclusion.

**Strength:** VERY STRONG - Regulatory requirements are real, choice is constrained

## Our Response to the Steelman

### Rebuttal 1: Regulatory Necessity ≠ Evidence of Actual Choice

The counter-argument assumes regulatory necessity logically implies PRAGMATIST classification. But:

**Problem:** We don't have evidence of which path BBVA actually chose.
- PRAGMATIST requires evidence of vendor relationship, DTCC connection, or compliance statement
- We have none of these
- Regulatory necessity only proves they MUST comply, not HOW they comply

**Counter-to-this:** Fair point, but in binary choice with no CDM evidence visible, traditional path is the rational inference.

---

### Rebuttal 2: Exhaustive Search ≠ Certainty of Non-Adoption

The counter-argument claims null results prove non-adoption. But:

**Problem:** Silent implementation is unfalsifiable
- Banks can implement CDM internally without announcements
- BBVA could have quiet CDM team in Madrid
- No hiring signals ≠ no skilled staff reassigned

**Counter-to-this:** Possible but increasingly unlikely with multiple null categories (no hiring, no blogs, no vendor hints).

---

### Rebuttal 3: Peer Pattern is Weak Evidence

The counter-argument uses Santander as proof of Spanish pattern. But:

**Problem:** One peer doesn't establish pattern
- Santander has SOME historical DRR signal; BBVA has none
- Insufficient data for robust pattern inference
- Spanish banks may differ in strategy

**Counter-to-this:** Fair objection, but pattern direction is consistent (both lacking CDM signals).

---

### Rebuttal 4: DTCC/Vendor Inference is Still Inference

The counter-argument infers DTCC relationship to justify PRAGMATIST. But:

**Problem:** This commits the same error we critique
- If CDM non-adoption can't be inferred from null evidence
- Then DTCC adoption can't be inferred from absence either
- Inference requires evidence, not just logical necessity

**Counter-to-this:** Valid objection. PRAGMATIST classification would also require vendor evidence to be stronger.

---

### Rebuttal 5: Null Evidence Has Limits

The counter-argument claims eight null results are conclusive. But:

**Problem:** Search methodology matters
- Did we check all possible vendor partnerships?
- Did we search for internal announcements?
- Could evidence exist in non-English sources?

**Counter-to-this:** Reasonable caveat, but Tier 1 search was comprehensive in scope and language.
