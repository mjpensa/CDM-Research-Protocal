## Key Findings

### 1. Custody Business Model = Low CDM Relevance

Both custody banks share the same fundamental characteristic:
- **Core business**: Asset custody and servicing for institutional clients
- **Derivatives role**: Supporting client activity, not proprietary trading
- **CDM relevance**: Low - derivatives not core to business model

### 2. Different CDM Dynamics Than Investment Banks

| Factor | Investment Banks | Custody Banks |
|--------|------------------|---------------|
| Derivatives Role | Core trading | Client support |
| CDM Motivation | Regulatory compliance, efficiency | Client service (if any) |
| Build vs Buy | Some build (JPMorgan) | Always buy |
| Expected Classification | Mixed | PRAGMATIST |

### 3. Infrastructure Role Doesn't Drive CDM

BNY Mellon has market infrastructure services (collateral management) but:
- **DTCC** is primary US derivatives infrastructure provider
- No evidence of CDM-specific infrastructure initiatives
- Infrastructure services don't override custody business model

### 4. Custody Bank Pattern Established

Phase 9 establishes clear pattern:
- Custody banks are PRAGMATIST regardless of G-SIB status
- Client service would use vendor solutions, not internal CDM build
- Low confidence ceiling (85%) due to limited evidence expected
