## Executive Summary

This research protocol assessed 31 international banks for their ISDA Common Domain Model (CDM) adoption posture. The analysis classifies banks as either **ARCHITECT** (active CDM adopters/contributors) or **PRAGMATIST** (vendor-dependent, non-CDM path).

### Key Finding

**CDM adoption remains rare**: Only 5 of 31 banks (16%) classified as ARCHITECT. The remaining 84% will follow vendor-provided CDM solutions when regulatory pressure requires.

## Key Patterns Identified

### 1. ARCHITECT Archetypes

Five distinct ARCHITECT patterns:

| Archetype | Characteristic | Example Banks |
|-----------|----------------|---------------|
| **Native** | Built CDM from ground up | UBS, Pictet, JPMorgan |
| **Leader** | Early contributor, shapes standards | Barclays |
| **Follower** | Adopts CDM for strategic market access | Standard Chartered |

### 2. PRAGMATIST Archetypes

All PRAGMATIST banks share "Vendor-Dependent" sub-classification but vary by driver:

| Driver | Characteristic | Example Banks |
|--------|----------------|---------------|
| **Restructuring** | Competing priorities | Deutsche Bank, UniCredit, Citigroup |
| **Retail Focus** | Derivatives not core | ING, Lloyds, Santander, Bank of America |
| **Regulatory Cohort** | Follow local regulator | Japanese Big Four, Chinese Big Four |
| **Custody Model** | Asset servicing focus | State Street, BNY Mellon |
| **No CDM Signals** | Derivatives-heavy but silent | Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley |

### 3. US Market: JPMorgan Stands Alone

Unlike European markets with multiple ARCHITECTs, US shows:
- **JPMorgan**: Only US bank in CDM production
- **Goldman Sachs**: PRAGMATIST (no CDM signals despite derivatives focus)
- **Morgan Stanley**: PRAGMATIST (wealth management dilution)
- **US G-SIB Adoption**: 1 of 7 (14%)
