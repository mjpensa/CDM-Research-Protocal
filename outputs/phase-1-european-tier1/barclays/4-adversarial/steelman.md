## Purpose
This document presents the **strongest possible case** for the classification of Barclays as ARCHITECT-Follower, synthesizing all evidence to create an irrefutable argument.

## The Steelman Argument

### Thesis
Barclays is the **archetypal ARCHITECT-Follower**: deeply engaged with CDM intellectually and strategically, but explicitly committed to following market infrastructure adoption rather than leading with production deployment. This is not a failure to achieve Leader status; it is a **deliberate strategic choice** documented in Barclays' own public statements.

---

## Evidence Pillar 1: Self-Identified Follower Strategy

### The "FMI-First" Position
Barclays has **publicly articulated** that market infrastructure providers (FMIs, CCPs, exchanges) should deploy CDM first, with banks following.

**Source**: Risk.net, "Common domain model needs infrastructure push, says Barclays"

**Key Statement**: Barclays advocates that exchanges and clearing houses should "take a lead role in delivering ISDA's CDM to the market."

### Interpretation
This is not a casual observation. This is **strategic positioning** that:
1. Acknowledges CDM value (Architect commitment)
2. Defines banks as **second movers** (Follower strategy)
3. Places production responsibility on FMIs (not self)

### Steelman Strength
When a bank **explicitly states** they will follow FMI adoption, classifying them as "Follower" is not inference - it is **listening to what they said**.

### What Barclays Invests In

3. **Working Groups**: Internal exploration, not deployment
4. **Rise Facilities**: Hosting industry events

### What Barclays Does NOT Invest In

2. **Governance positions**: No steering committee or maintainer roles

Investment in **soft infrastructure** (education, research, community) without investment in **hard infrastructure** (production, governance) is the definitional pattern of **Follower** behavior.
