# Steelman Arguments: HSBC Holdings PLC

## Research Date
2025-12-19

## Purpose
Present the strongest possible version of both classification arguments to ensure fair evaluation.

---

## STEELMAN: PRAGMATIST (Vendor-dependent)

### Core Argument
HSBC has made a strategic decision to not build internal CDM capability, instead outsourcing this entirely to Delta Capita/Fragmos Chain. This is a rational response to their competitive position and cost structure.

### Strongest Evidence Points

**1. Comprehensive Global Outsourcing (January 2025)**

The Delta Capita agreement is the definitive evidence:
- Scope: "OTC derivatives confirmation and settlement services globally"
- Duration: Multi-year agreement
- Coverage: Both confirmation AND settlement
- Geography: Global, not regional pilot

This is not component sourcing; it's complete outsourcing of CDM-related post-trade capability. If HSBC had internal CDM capability, this agreement would be unnecessary or limited in scope.

**2. The 6-Year Gap (2019-2025)**

The timeline is damning:
- 2018-2019: Participated in UK FCA/BoE DRR pilot using ISDA CDM
- 2020-2024: No visible CDM activity whatsoever
- 2025: Outsourced CDM capability to external vendor

If pilot participation created internal expertise, HSBC had 6 years to implement it. Instead, they waited until 2025 and then outsourced entirely. This gap demonstrates that pilot participation was exploratory, not foundational.

**3. Broader Outsourcing Strategy**

HSBC is considering outsourcing fixed-income trading operations to Citadel Securities and Jane Street (Bloomberg, March 2025). This is part of a systematic strategy to:
- Reduce IT costs
- Optimize operations
- Compete with tech-focused non-bank market makers

CDM outsourcing via Delta Capita fits this pattern perfectly. HSBC is choosing operational efficiency over technology investment across multiple domains.

**4. Pattern of Null Results**

What was NOT found is as significant as what was found:
- No internal CDM teams
- No CDM-related hiring
- No CDM contributions to ISDA or FINOS working groups
- No CDM technology patents
- No post-pilot implementation
- No CDM content on HSBC.com
- No Asian CDM leadership

For a bank of HSBC's size and derivatives market presence, this pattern of absence is highly informative. An ARCHITECT bank would have visible signals in at least some of these areas.

**5. Industry Engagement Without Technical Contribution**

HSBC had an executive (Jeroen Krens) serve as ISDA Board Chair. Despite this high-level industry engagement:
- No evidence of HSBC on CDM technical working groups
- No evidence of HSBC contributions to CDM specification
- Governance role without technical follow-through

This pattern is characteristic of PRAGMATIST: engage at governance level for market presence, but don't invest in technical capability.

### Strategic Rationale (Why PRAGMATIST Makes Sense)

**1. Cost Efficiency**
- Internal CDM development is expensive
- Vendor solutions offer proven technology at lower total cost
- Consistent with overall cost optimization strategy

**2. Focus on Core Competencies**
- HSBC's strengths are in Asian markets, trade finance, global network
- Derivatives technology is not a differentiating capability for HSBC
- Outsourcing allows focus on actual competitive advantages

**3. Regulatory Compliance Without Overinvestment**
- CDM is primarily regulatory-driven (EMIR, DRR)
- Vendor solution meets compliance requirements
- No need to build beyond compliance

**4. Fast-Follower Benefits**
- Let pioneers (Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley) make mistakes
- Adopt proven solutions when mature
- Reduce technology risk

### Conclusion
HSBC's approach is rational, consistent, and well-evidenced. The PRAGMATIST (Vendor-dependent) classification accurately captures their relationship with CDM.

## STEELMAN: ARCHITECT

### Core Argument
HSBC is strategically adopting CDM through a vendor partnership model, demonstrating sophisticated technology strategy rather than passive outsourcing. The absence of visible internal development does not mean absence of strategic intent.

### Strongest Evidence Points

**1. Strategic Vendor Selection**

HSBC specifically chose a CDM-native vendor (Delta Capita/Fragmos Chain):
- Fragmos Chain is "the only ISDA CDM-native post-trade platform"
- This is not default vendor selection; it's intentional CDM adoption
- Shows awareness and prioritization of CDM as industry standard

An uninformed PRAGMATIST would select any vendor that meets functional requirements. HSBC specifically chose CDM-native technology.

**2. Pilot Participation Demonstrates Expertise**

The 2018-2019 DRR pilot created internal understanding:
- HSBC participated in converting regulation into code using CDM
- Technical staff gained hands-on CDM experience
- This expertise informed vendor selection and integration strategy

The pilot wasn't wasted; it created the knowledge base to make informed technology decisions.

**3. Integration Requires Internal Capability**

Integrating Delta Capita's CDM solution with HSBC's systems requires:
- Understanding of CDM data model
- Integration architecture expertise
- Operational knowledge of CDM workflows

This creates internal capability through use, even if development is external.

**4. Industry Governance is a Form of Architecture**

HSBC's ISDA Board Chair role is significant:
- Chair influences strategic direction of ISDA
- CDM is key ISDA initiative
- Governance shapes standards, even without direct technical contribution

ARCHITECT behavior includes shaping the industry context, not just building technology.

**5. Anchor Client Status Implies Partnership**

HSBC is described as "anchor client" for Elaris OTC:
- Anchor clients often influence platform development
- Multi-year agreement suggests deep partnership
- HSBC may be shaping CDM implementation through vendor relationship

This is more than customer relationship; it's strategic partnership.

### Strategic Rationale (Why ARCHITECT Makes Sense)

**1. Smart Architecture = Strategic Technology Decisions**

ARCHITECT doesn't require building everything internally. It means:
- Understanding the technology landscape
- Making strategic technology choices
- Positioning for future capability

HSBC's CDM-native vendor selection demonstrates this.

**2. Asia-First Strategy Requires Standards**

HSBC's focus on Asia requires:
- Interoperability across jurisdictions
- Standardized data models for cross-border operations
- CDM adoption supports this strategy

**3. Industry Leadership Through Governance**

HSBC shapes derivatives markets through:
- ISDA board participation
- Regulatory engagement (DRR pilot)
- Industry collaboration

This is ARCHITECT behavior at industry level.

**4. Build vs. Buy is False Dichotomy**

Modern technology strategy often combines:
- Internal architecture expertise
- External platform adoption
- Strategic vendor partnerships

HSBC may be building internal expertise while leveraging external platforms.

### Conclusion
HSBC's approach could be interpreted as strategic CDM adoption through partnership model, demonstrating sophisticated ARCHITECT thinking rather than passive PRAGMATIST outsourcing.

## COMPARATIVE ASSESSMENT

| Criterion | PRAGMATIST Steelman | ARCHITECT Steelman | Advantage |
|-----------|---------------------|---------------------|-----------|
| Evidence strength | Strong direct evidence | Inference and interpretation | PRAGMATIST |
| Logical coherence | Consistent pattern | Requires assumptions | PRAGMATIST |
| Counterevidence explained | Yes (Delta Capita, 6-year gap) | Partially (requires hidden intent) | PRAGMATIST |
| Predictive power | High (outsourcing continues) | Moderate (depends on hidden strategy) | PRAGMATIST |
| Occam's Razor | Simpler explanation | Requires additional assumptions | PRAGMATIST |

### Verdict on Steelman Competition

**PRAGMATIST steelman is stronger.**

**Reasoning**:
1. PRAGMATIST case relies on direct evidence (contracts, announcements, patterns)
2. ARCHITECT case relies on inference and interpretation of intent
3. PRAGMATIST case explains all evidence coherently
4. ARCHITECT case requires hidden factors not in evidence
5. Simplest explanation (Occam's Razor) favors PRAGMATIST

---

## Final Steelman Recommendation

**Maintain PRAGMATIST (Vendor-dependent) classification.**

The ARCHITECT steelman makes plausible arguments, but they are not sufficient to overcome the weight of direct evidence supporting PRAGMATIST classification. Strategic vendor selection is consistent with informed PRAGMATIST behavior; it does not require ARCHITECT classification.
