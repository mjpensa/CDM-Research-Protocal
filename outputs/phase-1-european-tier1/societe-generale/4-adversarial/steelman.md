## The Steelman Case for ARCHITECT

Societe Generale's apparent absence from CDM adoption is likely a strategic communication choice rather than a reflection of actual technology positioning. Consider the unique position SocGen occupies: their Managing Director Eric Litvack served as ISDA Chairman for an unprecedented decade (2015-2024), the exact period encompassing CDM's entire lifecycle from conception through production deployment. Litvack didn't merely occupy this role - ISDA's announcement specifically credited him with driving "the shift to greater automation and digitization of derivatives markets." It strains credulity that the longest-serving chairman of the organization that developed CDM, who explicitly championed derivatives digitization, would not influence his home institution's technology direction.

The absence of public CDM announcements from SocGen should be understood in context. BNP Paribas sought first-mover advantage with their November 2022 CFTC DRR announcement, but SocGen's competitive positioning is fundamentally different. As the acknowledged global leader in equity derivatives - a distinction even BNP Paribas cannot claim - SocGen has no need to signal technology capability through press releases. Their market position speaks for itself. French corporate culture further explains the communication gap: unlike American banks that view technology announcements as competitive positioning, French institutions historically prefer to implement thoroughly before communicating. SocGen's EMIR Refit compliance (April 2024) was achieved without technology attribution because, from a French perspective, meeting regulatory deadlines is expected, not noteworthy. The implementation approach - whether CDM-based or otherwise - is considered internal technical detail.

The most compelling evidence for hidden ARCHITECT activity is the intersection of three factors: Litvack's governance position, EMIR Refit's complexity, and the vendor ecosystem. REGnosys, the CDM reference implementation provider, specifically targets large European banks and maintains confidential client relationships. SocGen could be consuming CDM through vendor channels without any public attribution - this is, in fact, how most enterprise software adoption works. The absence of FINOS contribution is consistent with this model: consuming CDM through REGnosys requires neither FINOS membership nor public contribution. The LinkedIn search finding no CDM engineers is explained by the vendor relationship - if REGnosys provides the implementation, SocGen doesn't need to hire CDM specialists internally. Rather than indicating absence of CDM adoption, the null results may indicate successful vendor delegation.

## Assessment of Steelman Strength

**Persuasiveness Level**: MODERATE

**Strongest Elements**:
- The Litvack paradox is genuinely anomalous and difficult to fully explain under PRAGMATIST hypothesis
- French corporate communication culture provides legitimate alternative explanation for announcement absence
- Vendor-mediated adoption through REGnosys is technically plausible and would be invisible to public searches

**Weakest Elements**:
- Relies entirely on hidden activity with zero supporting evidence
- The Litvack influence argument cuts both ways - if he prioritized CDM internally, why no evidence after 10 years?
- BNP Paribas (also French) publicly announced CDM - the "French culture" explanation is weakened
- 24+ months since BNP announcement without SocGen response exceeds reasonable "strategic timing" window
- REGnosys client relationships do get publicized for major wins (e.g., BNP Paribas was announced)

**Executive Response**: A skeptical executive would likely say: "This is plausible but unfalsifiable. You're asking me to believe in hidden activity with no evidence. The simplest explanation for comprehensive absence of CDM evidence is absence of CDM activity."

---

## Steelman Verdict

The steelman case presents a logically coherent alternative interpretation but lacks evidentiary support. The argument requires accepting multiple absence-explanations (French culture, vendor confidentiality, strategic timing) while discounting multiple absence-signals (FINOS, LinkedIn, ISDA announcements, EMIR attribution, vendor references).

The steelman does successfully identify one genuine weakness in the PRAGMATIST classification: the Litvack paradox remains unexplained. However, this paradox alone is insufficient to overturn comprehensive evidence of non-adoption.

**Conclusion**: The steelman is INSUFFICIENTLY PERSUASIVE to change classification but identifies legitimate uncertainty around the Litvack effect.
