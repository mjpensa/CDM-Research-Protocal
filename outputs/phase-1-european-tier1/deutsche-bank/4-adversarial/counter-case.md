## Devil's Advocate: Case FOR ARCHITECT

*Making the strongest possible case that Deutsche Bank is moving toward ARCHITECT status*

### Argument 1: Senior Industry Engagement Signals Intent

Dawd Haque is not a junior employee - he is the **Global lead for COO Regulatory Market Initiatives**. His participation in:
- Global Derivatives Digital Regulatory Reporting Group
- Industry Data Standards Committee (Chair)
- DTCC Global Trade Repository Steering Committee

...represents significant institutional investment. Deutsche Bank would not allocate a senior executive's time to these groups if they had no intention to adopt CDM. This level of engagement suggests:
- Strategic decision-making at board level
- Multi-year commitment to DRR standards
- Intent to adopt when standards mature

### Argument 2: DTCC Choice is Pragmatic, Not Permanent

Deutsche Bank's use of DTCC for EMIR Refit was a **tactical decision** driven by timeline:
- EMIR Refit deadline: April 2024
- DRR code availability: Evolving
- Risk calculus: Use proven infrastructure for compliance, adopt CDM later

This is actually the **rational ARCHITECT approach**: meet regulatory deadlines with existing infrastructure while building toward CDM adoption when standards are production-ready. Compare to BNP Paribas who had a 2+ year head start.

### Argument 3: €6.5B Controls Investment Creates Foundation

Deutsche Bank has invested over €6.5 billion in controls since 2019. This massive investment in data quality, reconciliation, and reporting infrastructure could be **preparatory** for CDM adoption:
- Cleaner data makes CDM translation easier
- Stronger controls reduce CDM implementation risk
- Investment capacity freed up post-2024 compliance cycle

### Argument 4: Competitive Pressure is Building

With JPMorgan in CDM production (October 2024) and BNP Paribas since Q3 2022, Deutsche Bank faces growing interoperability pressure from:
- Major counterparties expecting CDM connectivity
- Industry momentum toward standardization
- Regulatory encouragement of digital reporting

Deutsche Bank's industry group participation positions them to move quickly when competitive pressure intensifies.

### Conclusion (Devil's Advocate)

The evidence pattern is consistent with **ARCHITECT-in-waiting**: strategically positioned through industry engagement, pragmatically managing current compliance, and preparing for adoption when the business case is clearer.

**Probability Adjustment**: +10% → 57% ARCHITECT

## Steelman: Case FOR PRAGMATIST

*Making the strongest possible case that Deutsche Bank will remain PRAGMATIST*

### Argument 1: Industry Participation is Defensive, Not Offensive

Many banks participate in standards bodies to:
- Monitor developments that might affect them
- Influence standards to reduce future compliance costs
- Gather competitive intelligence

Dawd Haque's participation may be purely **defensive**: ensuring Deutsche Bank understands CDM/DRR without committing to adoption. Compare to firms that participate in ISO committees without implementing every standard.

### Argument 2: DTCC Relationship is Structural

Deutsche Bank's DTCC relationship for trade reporting is not a temporary bridge - it's a **strategic infrastructure choice**:
- DTCC is the dominant US and growing EU/UK infrastructure
- DTCC is CDM-enabled, allowing CDM adoption through the vendor
- No need to build internal CDM capability when vendor provides it

This suggests **PRAGMATIST (Vendor-Dependent)** rather than ARCHITECT.

### Argument 3: No Production Signals Despite Scale

Deutsche Bank is a major global derivatives dealer. If they were seriously pursuing CDM:
- We would see job postings for CDM engineers
- We would see conference presentations about their CDM journey
- We would see FINOS contributions or GitHub activity

The **complete absence** of these signals despite their scale and derivatives exposure suggests no active CDM build program.

### Argument 4: Regulatory Capacity Constraints

The €6.5 billion controls investment reflects **regulatory remediation priority**, not CDM preparation:
- AML/KYC compliance
- Risk management infrastructure
- Regulatory reporting fixes

This capacity consumption reduces available investment for CDM, which is optional enhancement rather than required remediation.

### Argument 5: 2022 Evidence is Stale

The JWG RegCast evidence is from 2022 - over 2.5 years ago. If that participation had led to CDM adoption, we would see more recent evidence of progress. The absence of current production/pilot signals suggests the 2022 engagement did not translate to action.

### Conclusion (Steelman)

The evidence pattern is consistent with **PRAGMATIST (Vendor-Dependent)**: meeting regulatory requirements through DTCC, monitoring industry developments through Dawd Haque, but not building internal CDM capability.

**Probability Adjustment**: -10% → 37% ARCHITECT
