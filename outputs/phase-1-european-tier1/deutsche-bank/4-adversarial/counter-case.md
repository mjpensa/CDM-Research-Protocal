## Devil's Advocate: Case for ARCHITECT

### Argument 1: Pilot-to-Production Gap May Be Normal
The 2020-2021 FINOS Legend pilot demonstrated Deutsche Bank's technical capability and willingness to engage with CDM. The 3-year gap between pilot and now (2024) may represent:
- Internal implementation time (CDM adoption is complex)
- Regulatory uncertainty (waiting for EMIR Refit finalization)
- Strategic patience (letting others de-risk the technology)

**Counter**: Major CDM adopters (Goldman Sachs, BNP Paribas, Barclays) announced production deployments 12-18 months after pilots, not 3+ years.

### Argument 2: German Regulatory Context
German banks face less immediate pressure than UK banks (post-Brexit) for CDM adoption. BaFin has not mandated CDM or DRR. Deutsche Bank may be:
- Waiting for clearer regulatory signals
- Prioritizing other compliance (MiCA, DORA)
- Planning future CDM adoption

**Counter**: EMIR Refit applies to all EU banks equally. Other European banks (BNP, ING) are actively adopting CDM for EMIR compliance.

### Argument 3: Russell Green's FINOS Role
Russell Green (MD, Head of Group Architecture) became FINOS Board Vice Chairman in 2021. This governance role suggests ongoing institutional commitment to CDM/FINOS even if not visible in public implementations.

**Counter**: Board roles are governance, not implementation. Green's role may be strategic positioning rather than active CDM deployment.

### Argument 4: Internal vs. External Visibility
Deutsche Bank may be implementing CDM internally without public announcements:
- Large banks often delay public disclosure of strategic technology
- CDM implementation could be in regulatory sandboxes
- Vendor partnerships may include NDAs preventing disclosure

**Counter**: If true, this would be unique among Tier 1 banks. All major CDM adopters (GS, MS, Barclays, BNP) have made public announcements.
