## Critical Evaluation of Steelman

### Steelman Strengths

1. **Empirically Grounded**: Uses real innovation programs, actual Bayesian probabilities
2. **Contextually Sophisticated**: Understands custodian business model nuances
3. **Methodologically Aware**: Recognizes search methodology limitations
4. **Statistically Informed**: 40.2% vs 55.0% is genuinely close
5. **Internally Coherent**: Premises build systematically

This is a **strong argument** that deserves serious consideration.

### Steelman Weaknesses

1. **Evidence-First Violation**: Entire argument is Tier 4 inference without Tier 1-3 evidence
2. **Private Channel Speculation**: Plausible scenarios are unverified hypotheses
3. **Innovation Program Inference**: Ascent/Dublin exist but show no CDM activity
4. **Circular Reasoning**: Assumes monitoring to prove monitoring
5. **OBSERVER Definition**: Protocol requires participation signal, not inferred awareness

### The Fatal Flaw

**Protocol Section 9 defines OBSERVER as**:
> "Working group member without technical artifacts"

**Evidence Required**:
- Membership signal (FINOS, ISDA, etc.) OR
- Participation signal (conference, job posting, employee discussion)

**BNY Mellon Evidence Across 22 Source Types**: **ZERO**

**The steelman argument conflates**:
- **Inferred awareness** (plausible based on scale + innovation)
- **Verified participation** (required by protocol definition)

**Verdict**: The argument is strong for "BNY Mellon is probably aware of CDM" but insufficient for "BNY Mellon is a verified OBSERVER per protocol."
